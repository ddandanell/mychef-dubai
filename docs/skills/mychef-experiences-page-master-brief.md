## Purpose

This is the creative blueprint for the main myCHEF Experiences page. It is not a web build. It defines the exact visitor journey, final-facing copy, image placement, emotional reasoning, image-generation direction and credibility safeguards.

The page must make visitors feel understood before showing them a service. It begins with the person and the intention beneath the booking, then turns the ten core commercial categories into emotional worlds and adds the Dubai desert as a signature destination world in its own right.

---

## 1. Creative North Star

### The central idea

**Tell us what this moment needs to mean.**

People do not really buy a chef, a menu or a decorated table. They may be trying to express love, mark achievement, restore connection, make a confident impression, remove the burden of hosting, or give somebody a story worth remembering.

myCHEF begins with that human intention and translates it into a feasible culinary and hospitality plan.

### The page promise

Perhaps someone needs to feel celebrated. Perhaps people need time to reconnect. Perhaps you want to make a confident impression—or simply enjoy your own guests.

We begin there. Then we shape the chef, food, service and atmosphere around what the moment needs to say.

### Non-negotiable visual principle

Every image must feel like a memory witnessed from inside the experience, not an advertisement assembled around it.

**Image balance across the page:**

- **40%** human emotion and interaction
- **20%** host relief and behind-the-scenes care
- **15%** atmosphere and setting
- **15%** culinary craft
- **10%** finished food

## 2. Global Image Language

### Art direction

- Cinematic documentary photography with quiet luxury.
- Dubai should be recognisable through light, architecture, water and atmosphere—not through constant landmark clichés.
- Human reactions should be subtle: a breath held, eyes beginning to recognise something, a hand reaching across a table, a host finally sitting down.
- Use imperfect traces of life: a slightly turned chair, melting candle wax, a folded menu, flour on a child's fingers, a half-finished plate.
- Show a wide and believable range of ages, skin tones, family structures and professional guests appropriate to Dubai.
- Chefs, assistants and service professionals must appear dignified, skilled and calm.
- Wardrobe should be refined and natural. Avoid costume-like eveningwear unless the occasion genuinely requires it.
- Food must look achievable by a professional culinary team, not surreal or architecturally impossible.

### Technical baseline for generated concept images

Add this direction to every prompt unless a scene specifies otherwise:

> Ultra-realistic cinematic editorial photograph, observed human moment, restrained quiet luxury, natural skin texture, believable hands, authentic Dubai residence or location, layered practical lighting, subtle film grain, rich but natural colour, realistic food and service details, no visible brand logos, no text in image, no watermarks, no exaggerated smiles, no stock-photo posing, no excessive gold, no empty showroom atmosphere, no impossible table setting, 16:9 landscape master with safe central crop for 4:5 mobile.

### Truth labels

- AI-created or commissioned illustrative scenes: **Experience concept shown**
- AI-generated imagery where required by policy or publishing context: **Concept visual**
- Only approved real-event photography may be described as a real myCHEF experience.
- Never manufacture a testimonial, client reaction, chef history or past event.

## 3. The Complete Page Journey

The page contains six acts and twenty internal moments. The visitor experiences one story, not twenty website blocks.

### Client-facing simplicity layer

The twenty moments describe the complete editorial page, not twenty tasks for the visitor. The guided route must feel like three short questions and one answer.

**What the visitor initially sees**

1. Who is this for?
2. What should the moment say?
3. Where should it happen?
4. One best recommendation and two alternatives

That is the complete first journey.

The participation question, full experience universe, operational details and additional choices remain available only after the initial recommendation or when relevant.

### Strict simplicity rules

- Show one question per screen.
- Use no more than seven visible choices at once; place less common options under Something else.
- Show one primary recommendation, one more intimate alternative and one more spectacular or unexpected alternative.
- Never show all experience worlds or 30 products in one grid.
- Always place a practical commercial label beneath a creative world name.
- Keep **I already know what I need** visible from the hero.
- Preserve the visitor's answers when they move into an experience or add-on page.
- Reveal operational warnings only when they apply to the selected setting.
- On mobile, one primary action must remain visually dominant.

## 4. Image Continuity Requirements

### Character continuity

For any multi-image story, create a reference sheet first and reuse:

- The same face, approximate age and physical features
- The same wardrobe
- The same jewellery and hairstyle
- The same villa, yacht or kitchen geometry
- The same chef/team uniforms
- A believable progression of daylight and candle burn

Do not generate several attractive but unrelated people and call them one experience.

### Shot sequence for each flagship product page

1. Anticipation — The private work before arrival  
2. Arrival — The guest enters the world  
3. Creation — Chef and team bring the experience together  
4. Recognition — The person begins to understand the meaning  
5. Signature moment — The unique emotional or theatrical centre  
6. Afterglow — What remains when the formal experience has passed  

### Mobile crops

- Protect faces and meaningful hands inside the central 60% of landscape masters.
- Create dedicated 4:5 and 9:16 crops when a wide environment carries crucial information.
- Never crop chefs at the wrists during craft actions.
- Never crop a service professional so only an anonymous hand or tray remains.

## 5. Copy and Credibility Guardrails

- Say *designed to express love or appreciation*, not *guaranteed to make them feel loved*.
- Do not imply myCHEF owns or guarantees access to yachts, islands, desert sites, beaches, landmarks or private venues.
- Separate culinary/hospitality fees from charter, venue, specialist production, groceries and equipment where applicable.
- Do not state that myCHEF “handles everything.” Use: *We coordinate the culinary and hospitality elements confirmed in your proposal.*
- Do not publish price-free inspiration without also providing an accessible route to budget guidance or a quote.
- Do not invent chef awards, client histories, reviews or case studies.
- Do not use nationality as proof of culinary competence.
- Do not treat workers as visual props. Show judgment, skill, communication and care.
- Do not retain client preferences without permission.
- Do not use protected medical claims for wellness services.

## 6. Red-Team Review

### Final copy red team

- **Hurried client:** Hero explains who/what/three recommendations; I Already Know What I Need visible.
- **Emotionally motivated client:** Emotional bridge retained after clear commercial explanation.
- **Sceptical client:** No guaranteed reactions; partner/location boundaries explicit.
- **Luxury client:** Observation, memory, place, service — not exclusive/opulent/world-class filler.
- **Repetition:** Public copy prefers concrete words: person, table, evening, course, home, guests, fridge, arrival.

### Remaining risks before 10/10

- Approved real case photography and testimonials
- Verified chef and service-team profiles
- Deliverability classification for featured products
- Indicative budget or quote guidance
- Mobile/desktop prototype tested with real potential clients
- Consistent character sheets or commissioned photography

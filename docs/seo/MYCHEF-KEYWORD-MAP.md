# How to use this file

**This is a REFERENCE file. It is not a task list.**

For each URL it identifies the main keyword it owns, its supporting subkeywords, and what the previous round had targeted.

These keywords guided the approved page copy written alongside this map. That copy is delivered separately.

**Do not mechanically insert every listed keyword into the website.** The approved copy already carries its keywords. Adding more would recreate the exact-match keyword stuffing this project exists to remove.

Use this map for verification, future content work, checking what a page is meant to target, and avoiding cannibalisation.

**It is not a keyword-stuffing checklist.**

### How the keywords go into copy

- **The main keyword** belongs in four places only: title tag, H1, the first 100 words, and once in a subheading.
- **Subkeywords go inside sentences, never into headings.** A subkeyword earns its place when a sentence that had to be written anyway happens to contain it. If a sentence exists only to carry a term, delete the sentence.
- **The test:** read it aloud. If it sounds like something a person would say to a customer, it stays. If it sounds like it was written for a machine, it goes — however well it matches a keyword.

The site already carries 161 pages of exact-match subheadings appended by a previous SEO round, which are being deleted separately. Feeding this list into that same pattern would rebuild the problem with a shorter word list.

## What changed, and why

### 1. Fewer subkeywords, on purpose — 1,849 down to 984

The previous set averaged nearly nine per page. That is more than a page can carry without the copy bending around the terms, which is how the exact-match phrasing on the live site happened. Eight per page is the hard cap here, and many pages carry fewer because fewer is honest. No page exceeds the cap.

### 2. The homepage was targeting the wrong market

| | |
|---|---|
| **Previous main keyword** | `home catering dubai` |
| **Previous subkeywords included** | *cheap home catering dubai · affordable catering services in dubai · small party catering dubai indian · pakistani catering services dubai* |
| **New main keyword** | `private chef and catering dubai` |

The homepage of a business whose private chef service starts at **AED 700–950 per person** was pointed at *"cheap home catering dubai"*. That traffic cannot convert at myCHEF's rates, and copy written to serve it drags the brand's positioning down at the exact moment a visitor forms a first impression.

### 4. Nine pages have no keyword target — and that is the right answer

`/blog` · `/contact` · `/gallery` · `/press` · `/privacy-policy` · `/review` · `/site-map` · `/terms` · `/chefs/ahmed-executive-chef`

A contact form is not a ranking asset. Hub pages rank on brand and route traffic onward; assigning `/blog` a target would set it competing with the twenty posts it links to.

`/chefs/ahmed-executive-chef` is the interesting one: "executive chef dubai" is overwhelmingly a **recruitment** query — chefs seeking work, operators seeking staff — and myCHEF already serves that intent at `/become-a-mychef`. Pointing a customer-facing profile at it would pull job-seekers onto a booking page.

### 5. A structural gap was found: cuisine + private chef

The site has cuisine **catering** pages but no cuisine **private chef** pages, and those are different buyers at different price tiers — AED 90–150 event catering versus AED 700–950 private dining. The strings "middle eastern", "lebanese", "pastry" and "executive" appeared **zero times** across all 206 pages.

Three chef profiles now fill that gap:

**Important constraint:** every one of these targets a **service capability** the site already evidences elsewhere, not a claim about the chef.

The owner confirmed on 2026-08-02 (`APPROVED-CLAIMS.md`) that **Ahmed Al-Rashid, Marco Rossi, Sofia Moretti and Layla Hassan are real people**, so naming them is allowed and these pages are not blocked. Two limits still apply: the per-chef **"years of experience"** claims are not evidenced and must not be published, and `/about` names three people (Thomas Chen, Marco Adriano, Elena Vasquez) who are **not** on the confirmed roster — report those to the owner rather than rewriting that page from this map.

### 6. Main keyword collisions: 0

Every main keyword is now owned by exactly one page, and no subkeyword duplicates another page's main. This was not checked in the previous round; two collisions were found and fixed, including the homepage competing with `/private-chef-dubai` for "private chef dubai".

## How these keywords were derived — read this before relying on them

**There is no search-volume data in this file.** No Ahrefs, Semrush or Keyword Planner access was available. Nothing here is ranked by volume, difficulty or CPC, and no such claim is made.

### The review that produced it

Every one of the 920 subkeywords in the previous draft was read individually — not sampled — by one of eight reviewing agents, each covering one page group. Their proposals were then attacked by three independent adversarial verifiers, each briefed to default to rejection, working through a different lens:

- **search reality** — would a person in Dubai actually type this?
- **cannibalisation** — does this hand one page a keyword another page owns?
- **brand and price fit** — does this damage premium positioning or imply a product myCHEF does not offer?

**An addition survived only if no verifier rejected it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Subkeywords read individually | 920 |
| Judged sound as they stood | 559 |
| Additions proposed | 414 |
| **Additions rejected by at least one verifier** | **173** |
| Additions kept | 241 |
| Subkeywords removed | 188 |
| Subkeywords reworded | 172 |
| Main keywords corrected | 77 |
| Collisions found and fixed | 2 |

A separate three-agent review argued the case for and against targeting the 15 previously untargeted pages, with a third agent adjudicating. Six were promoted; nine were confirmed as correctly untargeted.

### What this does and does not prove

It means every keyword here was defended by one agent and attacked by three. That is a real quality bar, and it removed 60 defects the earlier automated checks could not see — including *"halal jain catering dubai"* (Jain dietary practice is not Islamic) and commercial price modifiers on a chef-recruitment page.

**It does not mean these keywords have proven demand.** Nobody in this process had volume data. Before a page receives real investment, check its terms in a keyword tool.

---

## On "will every page rank?"

**All 197 targeted pages now have a distinct main keyword that no other page claims** — that is the precondition, and it was not true before. Previously six pages competed for private-chef price intent and eight for romantic dining, so some of them could not rank however good the writing was.

What nobody can promise is that all 197 *will* rank. Three constraints are real:

1. **Six consolidation candidates are not expected to rank independently** — their intent belongs to a stronger page, and trying to rank all of them is what caused the cannibalisation in the first place.
2. **`/staff-meals-catering-dubai`** targets a market transacting near AED 10 a meal that myCHEF has no product for. It can rank; it will not convert.
3. **Seasonal pages rank seasonally.** Iftar, Ramadan, Christmas, NYE, Valentine's and National Day are judged in their month, not in August.

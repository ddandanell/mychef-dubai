To compare catering quotes in Dubai, begin by making sure they describe the same event. A lower per-person figure may cover food alone, while another proposal includes staff, tableware, setup and clearing. The useful comparison is the complete agreed service, with the same guest count, menu role, venue and duration.

This guide gives you a practical way to normalise proposals and identify questions before booking. The worked figures are fictional arithmetic examples, not myCHEF prices or market estimates. For current service options, start with [catering in Dubai](/catering-dubai) and the [published pricing guide](/dubai-catering-prices-guide).

## Compare catering quotes in Dubai on one event brief

Send every supplier the same core information: date, location, guest count, event type, service format, intended food times, dietary requirements and venue facilities. If one supplier receives a detailed brief and another receives only “dinner for twenty”, their proposals are unlikely to be directly comparable.

Specify whether you need a delivered meal, an actively maintained buffet, passed reception food or plated service. These are different jobs. Include whether drinks, tableware, furniture, styling or other event support should be quoted.

Describe access and kitchen constraints. A long route from loading to the event space, limited finishing facilities or a restricted setup window can affect the work required. Ask suppliers to state the assumptions they have made where information is missing.

Keep the brief version with each proposal. If the guest count or format changes, request an updated quote rather than comparing one current document with another based on an earlier event.

## Separate food quantity from menu length

A long menu is not necessarily a larger meal. Ask what quantity is included, how it is allocated and whether each guest is expected to receive every item. This is particularly important for canapés, sharing menus and buffet selections.

For plated dining, confirm the course structure and any choices guests may make. For sharing dishes, ask how portions are distributed and replenished. For a buffet, understand the intended service period and how the spread is maintained.

If a proposal uses a phrase such as “selection of bites”, ask for the varieties and quantities. The [canapé planning guide](/blog/canape-reception-planning-dubai) shows why total pieces, items per guest and number of varieties are separate questions.

Record substitutions and alternatives too. A vegetarian meal or another confirmed requirement should be specified clearly enough to assess, rather than left as “available on request” when the guest is already known to need it.

## Break the service into comparable lines

Create a simple comparison sheet using the categories below. A line can be included, separately priced, supplied by the venue or not required. The important point is to know which answer applies.

| Category | What to compare | Common question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Menu, quantities, ingredients and preparation scope | Is this a full meal or one stage of the event? |
| Kitchen | Chefs, assistants and finishing equipment | What must the venue provide? |
| Service | Waiting staff, drinks staff, runners and coordination | Which tasks and hours are covered? |
| Tableware | Plates, cutlery, glasses, serving pieces and linen | Are replacements, washing and collection included? |
| Logistics | Delivery, loading, access and transport | What assumptions could change the charge? |
| Setup and clearing | Defined work before and after guests | Where does the supplier’s responsibility end? |
| Optional items | Styling, furniture or other requested support | Can each option be accepted or declined separately? |
| Tax and terms | Itemised treatment and payment conditions | What is the complete payable total and when is it due? |

Do not add a cost for an item you do not need simply to make every quote look identical. Instead, identify the complete service your event requires and compare each supplier against that specification.

## A fictional example of why the headline can mislead

Imagine two proposals for the same twenty-person event. Proposal A lists food at AED 240 per guest, while Proposal B lists food at AED 250 per guest. Looking only at those figures makes A appear lower.

| Fictional line item | Proposal A | Proposal B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food for twenty | AED 4,800 | AED 5,000 |
| Agreed service staff | AED 1,200 | Included in the stated scope |
| Required tableware | AED 600 | AED 700 |
| Delivery | AED 200 | AED 100 |
| Comparable subtotal before tax | AED 6,800 | AED 5,800 |

Under these invented assumptions, B has the lower subtotal for the specified service. That does not make B better in every respect: you still need to compare the menu, staff roles, hours, equipment and terms. It simply shows why the per-person food line is not the entire decision.

Do not use these figures as a price expectation. Replace every number and inclusion with the actual written proposals for your event, then confirm the applicable tax and final payable amount with the supplier.

## Ask what “staff included” means

Request roles and working periods. A chef preparing food, a server attending tables and a person managing drinks perform different tasks. A headcount without responsibilities leaves room for assumptions.

Confirm whether the stated hours include setup and clear-down or only guest service. If guests arrive at 7pm, the team may need access earlier; if dinner ends at 10pm, clearing may continue afterwards. The proposal should make the service period understandable.

Ask how an extension is handled and whether availability must be confirmed. Do not assume personnel can remain indefinitely because the event runs late. Equally, do not pay for an undefined extension without knowing what work it covers.

## Check the venue assumptions

Ask each supplier what equipment, surfaces, power, water, storage and access it expects the venue to provide. Confirm those assumptions with the authorised property contact before accepting the proposal.

If a supplier assumes a full working kitchen and the venue has only a small pantry, the quote may need revision. If the venue already supplies suitable tableware, a separate hire line may be unnecessary. The same item can be essential in one location and redundant in another.

For outdoor events, confirm the equipment and fallback plan. The [outdoor kitchen guide](/blog/outdoor-villa-catering-kitchen-dubai) helps identify the questions, while the supplier and property team decide what is suitable.

Do not treat a site visit or equipment assessment as a formality if it is needed to finalise the scope. Ask which parts of the quote remain provisional until that review is complete.

## Make dietary scope visible

List the known requirements in the brief and ask suppliers to confirm what they can accommodate. Compare the proposed dishes and service process, not just whether a tick box says “dietary options”.

Ask whether alternatives are included in the main price or separately quoted, and how changes after the confirmation deadline are handled. Essential ingredient and handling questions need direct confirmation from the food business.

If a proposal cannot accommodate a requirement, that is a meaningful limitation to consider. Do not average it away because the total is attractive or assume it can be fixed casually on the day.

The [mixed-diet menu guide](/blog/mixed-diet-dinner-party-dubai) helps you describe a complete alternative plate. Use it to improve the brief, then obtain the supplier’s actual confirmation for the event.

## Distinguish fixed and variable charges

Some costs may change with guest count, while others relate to the event as a whole. Ask which lines are per person, per item, per hour or fixed for the agreed scope. This makes later revisions easier to understand.

A reduction in guests may reduce food quantities without removing the need for a chef, vehicle or particular equipment. An increase may require more than additional portions if the service format or room needs another staff role. Ask the supplier to explain the thresholds relevant to your event rather than assuming all charges move proportionally.

If attendance is uncertain, request a clear method for finalising numbers. Keep estimated attendance, the confirmed order and the chargeable minimum distinct. The written terms should state the applicable deadlines and conditions.

Avoid comparing one supplier’s starting price with another’s final tailored proposal. A starting figure is a point of reference; the complete event specification is what you can actually assess and accept.

## Read payment and change terms alongside the menu

Confirm the payment schedule, what constitutes a confirmed booking and any deadlines for menu or guest-count changes. Keep the terms with the proposal rather than treating them as a separate administrative detail.

Ask how cancellation, postponement, supplier changes or unavailable ingredients are handled under the agreement. This guide does not interpret legal rights; it identifies practical questions to resolve in the supplier’s written terms before you book.

If your company requires a purchase order or invoice approval, tell the supplier early. Internal administration should fit the booking timetable so there is no misunderstanding about whether the date is actually reserved.

Do not rely on a verbal assurance that contradicts the document. Ask for the relevant clarification in writing and make sure the final accepted version contains the agreed scope.

## Compare presentation with evidence

Ask what the sample images represent: the supplier’s completed work, a style reference or an illustrative concept. A photograph can show a direction, but it does not by itself confirm the exact equipment, portions or staffing included in your proposal.

If a particular presentation matters, name the elements you expect and have them specified. “Like the picture” can mean the food, tableware, flowers, furniture or the entire setting to different people.

For a wedding or a menu where a tasting is offered, use it to record specific decisions. The [wedding tasting guide](/blog/wedding-menu-tasting-dubai) explains how to connect the food evaluation with the final event plan.

Avoid assuming a supplier has delivered your exact event format simply because its images are attractive. Ask relevant questions about the proposed process and scope, and judge the clarity of the answers.

## Use a short clarification message

> Thank you for the proposal. Please confirm the total for the attached event brief, including the final guest count, menu quantities, staff roles and working hours, required equipment and tableware, delivery, setup, clear-down, collection and itemised tax. Please identify anything the venue or host must provide, any provisional assumptions, the deadline for final changes and the applicable booking terms. Please show optional items separately so we can approve them clearly.

Send the same clarification to each supplier where relevant. Keep the tone factual. The aim is to understand the service, not to turn a comparison into a request for every supplier to imitate another business’s menu or pricing.

Once the replies arrive, update the comparison sheet and resolve material gaps. A missing essential detail is more important than a small difference in the headline figure.

## Make the final decision on the complete fit

Consider the food, service plan, venue fit, communication, confirmed requirements and total cost together. A clear proposal with realistic responsibilities is easier to manage than an attractive number surrounded by assumptions.

When you accept, identify the exact version and keep any later changes documented. Give the event team one final brief so the booking conversation and the operational plan remain aligned.
